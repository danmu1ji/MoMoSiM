# Toki — Relationship Story 3

Original English wiki story transcript. No Korean dialogue is translated.

Source: https://bluearchive.wiki/wiki/Toki/Relationship_Story/3
Status: source-extracted-unreviewed


> Schale Executive Office
> Toki and I began work on the day's duties at Schale.
- "Toki."
- Toki (C&C): What is it, Sensei?
- "You seem used to this kind of work."
- Toki (C&C): Perhaps. I'm not sure what you mean by "this kind of work."
- "Just what we're doing now—organizing files and materials, that kind of thing."
- Toki (C&C): Ah, I see. Well, it may be insensitive of me to bring this up, but this was frequently among the duties assigned to me by the person I used to assist.
- Toki (C&C): Of course, I am also perfectly capable of cooking, cleaning, or any other housekeeping you might require.
- Toki (C&C): Hm. You're referring to my work for Schale?
- Toki (C&C): I'm not sure that I agree. Sometimes, there's so little work that I feel a bit awkward.
- Toki (C&C): I am a superior maid. You may assign me more duties if you wish.
- Toki (C&C): For example, I am escorting you now, but I am also capable of repelling enemies, suppressing crowds, defusing explosives, tracking, annihilating, intercepting, destroying, and exterminating. Please call on me if you need any of those services.
- "But none of those things have anything to do with being a maid?!"
- Toki (C&C): Perhaps not. And yet, going above and beyond is one of the qualities of a superior maid.
- "I-Is that so?"
- Toki (C&C): It is. As a perfect maid, you can trust me to give you correct information.
- Toki (C&C): ...Hobby? Time off?
- Toki (C&C): I'm sorry, I'm not sure what you're talking about. Please explain the concept to me.
- Toki (C&C): Something you do when you have time to spare? Are you testing me by asking such difficult questions?
- Toki (C&C): I generally worked through the weekend. I very rarely had time off from my duties.
- Toki (C&C): S-Sensei?
- Toki (C&C): What do you mean, you can't let this pass? Oh, are we going somewhere right now? Sensei? I said before, you can't just drag me around by the arm—
> Downtown Kivotos
- "So, how'd you like the movie?"
- Toki (C&C): I didn't understand it at all.
- Toki (C&C): Why did the characters begin shooting in the middle of a chase? In fact, what reason was there to escalate the situation to a shootout at all?
- Toki (C&C): If they had simply handled the situation better from the beginning, instead of acting so clearly suspicious, the incident would've ended without anyone being hurt.
- "But that's...the core concept of the screenplay..."
- Toki (C&C): I noticed many other flaws. For example...
> Toki then spent more than half an hour outlining the film's many inconsistencies.
- Toki (C&C): ...That concludes my report.
- "...Great work."
- Toki (C&C): It was nothing.
- Toki (C&C): Oh? I see. You're saying that generally, a movie is simply enjoyed, and one doesn't typically spend so much time nitpicking it.
- Toki (C&C): It seems that I still need training in the art of watching a movie. In the future, I'll put more effort in.
> Shopping Street
- "This time, let's try manga..."
- Toki (C&C): I've heard the saying, "a picture is worth a thousand words," but I'm not sure I agree.
- Toki (C&C): My qualm is this. When it comes to information density and efficiency, is this method truly the best?
- Toki (C&C): Despite what people say, I think the content of this image could be conveyed far more densely in a written medium, with less expenditure of ink and paper. In terms of efficiently conveying information, I believe a video medium to be superior.
- Toki (C&C): So... What is intended by using the picture format? Not only does it have the problems that I already described, but it takes so much time and effort to create.
- Toki (C&C): I assume there must be some reason, but it eludes me.
- Toki (C&C): Shopping is truly an intricate ritual.
- Toki (C&C): You make the decision, with knowledge and intent, to buy so many unnecessary things.
- Toki (C&C): I'm not sure I understand. All acts must have meaning, don't you think?
- Toki (C&C): And yet shopping doesn't seem to have any.
- "So you didn't enjoy it after all..."
> I continued to try out a number of hobbies with Toki.
> Toki showed interest in exactly...none of them.
> Downtown Kivotos
- "Good work... Toki..."
- Toki (C&C): I did nothing difficult or praiseworthy.
- Toki (C&C): In fact, I believe you've done most of the work today.
- Toki (C&C): ...
- Toki (C&C): Um, Sensei?
- "Hm?"
- Toki (C&C): I'm still not sure...what sort of thing a hobby is.
- Toki (C&C): Is it a technical skill that requires mastery, such as the skill of escorting someone?
- Toki (C&C): Is it an activity that requires success and understanding, or something of that nature?
> Toki was looking at me with an uncharacteristic look of anxiety.
> I chose my words carefully.
- "A hobby is..."
- Toki (C&C): Yes?
- Toki (C&C): Something that I enjoy... Something I do just because I want to.
- Toki (C&C): ...
- Toki (C&C): So, my hobby could be...
- Toki (C&C): ...Heehee.
- "What? Did you already think of something?"
- Toki (C&C): It's nothing! Nevermind.
- Toki (C&C): Then, Sensei.
- Toki (C&C): Should we go back?
- Toki (C&C): I'll escort you back to Schale.
- "It seems like there's something you're not telling me..."
- Toki (C&C): Hm, I wonder. What could that be?
- "You're really not going to tell me?"
- Toki (C&C): Well, how could I tell you when I hardly know myself?
- "Nice diversion."
> Toki and I chatted and walked back to Schale together.
