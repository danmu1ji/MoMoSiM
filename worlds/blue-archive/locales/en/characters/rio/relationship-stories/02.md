- Rio (Seminar): Not without giving away the hideout.
- "And that's why you used Schale as a forwarding address."
- Rio (Seminar): Exactly, yes. So for that, my apologies.
- "Back up for a sec: you have a hideout?"
- Rio (Seminar): More than one.
- Rio (Seminar): At the moment, I'm using the one with the most optimal location...
- Rio (Seminar): ...but I do have several backups if the need arises.
- Rio (Seminar): One whiff of trouble, and my AMAS will take me straight to the next one.
- "Sounds like a rough way to live."
- Rio (Seminar): I'd be lying if I said it wasn't...
- Rio (Seminar): But still, it's rational.
- Rio (Seminar): ...You think it sounds inefficient?
- Rio (Seminar): ...Well, maybe.
- Rio (Seminar): Eimi and I talked it over a while ago.
- Rio (Seminar): She says I'm the type of person who can give up efficiency for rationality.
- Rio (Seminar): With her, she told me, it's the other way around.
- Rio (Seminar): I thought you couldn't have one without the other, honestly. I thought the most efficient option would naturally be the rational one.
- Rio (Seminar): But...apparently, that's not the case.
- Rio (Seminar): People can't always be rational, and most of the events in our lives are between people and other people.
- Rio (Seminar): The way I used to see that, I thought people were unreliable and untrustworthy. That's why I thought everything had to be controlled.
- Rio (Seminar): ...I don't think that's the case anymore.
- Rio (Seminar): Because people can't be rolled back.
- "Rolled back?"
- Rio (Seminar): Yes.
- Rio (Seminar): I've thought a lot about that lately.
- Rio (Seminar): People aren't like machines.
- Rio (Seminar): If a machine gets a broken part, you can just replace it. If an AI's decision-making algorithm goes wrong, you can just rewrite it.
- Rio (Seminar): You can make them good as new. Just like they were before. Every part of them is replaceable.
- Rio (Seminar): But...people aren't. Things that go wrong for them can't be fixed that way.
- Rio (Seminar): Some parts of a person can heal on their own, but there are limits to that.
- Rio (Seminar): About 330 billion cells are born and die in every human body. The cellular turnover rate is anywhere from a month to a year...
- Rio (Seminar): But a brain's nerve cells, for example, don't get replaced at any point in your lifespan.
- Rio (Seminar): Do you know the story of the Ship of Theseus?
- Rio (Seminar): It's a story that illustrates what it means, or doesn't mean, to replace things piece by piece.
- Rio (Seminar): And whether it's the same thing after it's all been replaced.
- Rio (Seminar): Let's say you have a ship. As time goes on, parts of the ship need replacing.
- Rio (Seminar): And so you keep replacing parts, until one day, every single part's been replaced at one time or another. Is it still the same ship, or is it not?
- Rio (Seminar): I think...you can apply that concept to machines.
- Rio (Seminar): Even if you replace every part, then as long as it keeps functioning, it's the same machine as it ever was.
- Rio (Seminar): ...But I don't think humans work the same way.
- Rio (Seminar): Maybe someday in the far future, they'll have ways of analyzing every neuron in your brain, and creating an exact carbon and protein-based copy of anything you need replaced...
- Rio (Seminar): But I don't want to do that thought experiment right now. It requires a lot of assumptions, and doesn't have much bearing on reality.
- Rio (Seminar): You know what I mean by "thought experiment," right? When you sort of simplify a scenario, and then make predictions based off of that?
- Rio (Seminar): There are really two types of thought experiments.
- Rio (Seminar): One is where you can predict near-accurate results based on precise simulations.
- Rio (Seminar): And the other is built off of imagination and presumptions.
- Rio (Seminar): The question of the human "ship of Theseus" is definitely one of the latter.
- Rio (Seminar): So I don't see much use in considering that right now.
- Rio (Seminar): In other words, Sensei...
- Rio (Seminar): I wanted to say that you, the way you are now...
- Rio (Seminar): ...are irreplaceable for me.
- "Hm? What do you mean?"
- Rio (Seminar): ...Well, it's all right if you don't understand.
- Rio (Seminar): But one day...
- Rio (Seminar): ...I hope you'll see me that way too.
- Rio (Seminar): Just a thought I had in the moment.
- Rio (Seminar): Now then, since I've picked up all my packages, I should get back.
- "...How do you plan on going back?"
- Rio (Seminar): Just like I did on the way here, jamming all the traffic cams and CCTVs.
- Rio (Seminar): So no one sees me leaving Schale and —
- Rio (Seminar): Huh? B-But...
- "President Rio, please listen to Sensei."
- Rio (Seminar): A-All right...
