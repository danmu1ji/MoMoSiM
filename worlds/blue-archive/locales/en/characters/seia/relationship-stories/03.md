# Seia — Relationship Story 3

Original English wiki story transcript. No Korean dialogue is translated.

Source: https://bluearchive.wiki/wiki/Seia/Relationship_Story/3
Status: source-extracted-unreviewed


- Seia (Tea Party): ...Ah, Sensei.
- Seia (Tea Party): What brings you here? I don't believe I specifically asked for you to come...
- Seia (Tea Party): No, of course I'm happy. Your visits are always a pleasure.
- Seia (Tea Party): In fact, it's wonderful to see you here unprompted.
- Seia (Tea Party): As it happens, I am...here by myself all day.
- Seia (Tea Party): ...Fufu, indeed.
- Seia (Tea Party): This is the exclusive property of Trinity's Tea Party. Only members and their guests are welcome here.
- Seia (Tea Party): In fact, it's not the only Tea Party-exclusive building on campus.
- Seia (Tea Party): There are more than just the three of us in the Tea Party, after all. There are many of us, in fact.
- Seia (Tea Party): Regardless, as you can see, this place is very quiet.
- Seia (Tea Party): A few students do drop by from time to time, but they typically come for class materials and such.
- Seia (Tea Party): Not many of them read for pleasure, and those who do tend to read in their own rooms.
- Seia (Tea Party): You ask why I read here, then?
- Seia (Tea Party): Fufu, I made my room too comfortable for reading books. I rarely read there these days.
- Seia (Tea Party): Were I to try, I would likely nod off, and the book would slip from my hands.
- Seia (Tea Party): So when it comes to reading, a bit of discomfort can actually help.
- Seia (Tea Party): You haven't seen it from me yourself, but in truth, I read so much that people often check for me here before they try my own room.
- Seia (Tea Party): I would love to find others to share my books with...
- Seia (Tea Party): ...but even for such a widespread hobby, bringing people together for it can be extraordinarily difficult.
- Seia (Tea Party): And, were I to bring my peers to the library, I would inevitably feel as if I were burdening them.
- Seia (Tea Party): Another complication is my own social standing. It's difficult to discuss books freely when one of us has higher status.
- Seia (Tea Party): Were I to express my opinion of a book in front of regular students, they might feel compelled to agree.
- Seia (Tea Party): So I could never bring myself to follow through with such a thing. I've often thought about it, but I've always stopped myself.
- Seia (Tea Party): I would love to share conversations through a book, but without troubling anyone.
- Seia (Tea Party): ...I understand where you're leading me with that.
- Seia (Tea Party): But you needn't worry. My life is not lonely or lacking.
- Seia (Tea Party): My reading hobby is rather solitary, but I do have fine relationships with other students.
- Seia (Tea Party): That said... Um...
- Seia (Tea Party): Perhaps you might be willing to accompany me on a trip through a book sometime.
- Seia (Tea Party): We would both enjoy it. At least I think so.
- Seia (Tea Party): I wouldn't have to worry about dragging you about anyplace or burdening you with my own opinions.
- Seia (Tea Party): ...This already sounds greedy of me. But what would you say to it?
- Seia (Tea Party): It wouldn't be frequent, mind you, only occasional...but I would love to have you as a reading partner now and again.
- Seia (Tea Party): ...Fufu. Wonderful.
- Seia (Tea Party): Every day we meet, you find a new way to make me happy.
- Seia (Tea Party): Now, the question is, would you like to pick a book? Or would you like me to recommend you one?
- Seia (Tea Party): This place has at least one copy of every book known to the school.
- Seia (Tea Party): Unless you're looking for some ancient tome, in which case you'd want the old library, you can find it here.
- Seia (Tea Party): And if you ARE looking for something from the old library, I can give them a call for you.
- Seia (Tea Party): ...That's a joke. As I mentioned before, I wouldn't abuse my power that way. Not that our school's Library Committee president would let me.
- Seia (Tea Party): But I would like to recommend you a book. Several, actually, if you'd like to see this list I've prepared.
- Seia (Tea Party): ...Go on. Choose whichever one you like.
- Seia (Tea Party): Mmm, mm. I see. So you're going with...that one.
- Seia (Tea Party): ...Ah.
- Seia (Tea Party): N-Nothing. Merely a...minor problem.
- Seia (Tea Party): That book isn't here right now. Someone already borrowed it.
- Seia (Tea Party): And that someone is me. The book is in my room.
- Seia (Tea Party): ...Now why are you looking at me like that? Do you not believe me?
- Seia (Tea Party): I said I RARELY read books in my room, not that I NEVER do. I promise you, it's there as we speak.
- Seia (Tea Party): And, by that happy coincidence...
- Seia (Tea Party): I suppose we'll have to head back to my room so that I can give it to you.
- Seia (Tea Party): Again, this is pure coincidence. A lucky, delightful coincidence.
- Seia (Tea Party): Lucky, also, that I happened to think of borrowing more books today...
- Seia (Tea Party): Fufu, well, let's just say this coincidence isn't the only stroke of luck that could have happened here.
- Seia (Tea Party): There's always the chance it could happen again. One must be prepared for such things.
- Seia (Tea Party): That said, I do believe I've checked out too many books to carry.
- Seia (Tea Party): ...Sensei?
- Seia (Tea Party): Would you mind helping me?
> Afterwards, I carried Seia's books to her room.
> The "recommended book" from Seia was on the table, as if it was waiting for me.
