> Red Winter Third Shared Cafeteria Kitchen
- "Soybean sprouts instead of mung bean sprouts?"
- Takane (Publishing Department): Yes. Mung beans obviously grow into mung bean plants.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Soybean sprouts grow into soy bean plants.
- Takane (Publishing Department): I believe that the mung bean variety is smoother and has a cleaner flavor, but...
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...Because mung beans cook so quickly, they can become quite unappetizing within seconds.
- "So what is this 'Bean Sprout Festival'...?"
- Takane (Publishing Department): As for that name... It's something that I created on a bit of a whim.
- Takane (Publishing Department): It simply means that on every other Wednesday, I have a habit of grilling up soybean sprouts on a hot griddle.
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...Please don't ask me if I didn't know how to make any other dishes...
- Takane (Publishing Department): Since we're already at it, why don't we begin a critique on soybean sprouts and mung bean sprouts?
- Takane (Publishing Department): I already told you how each ingredient is prepared, so we can skip that.
- Takane (Publishing Department): The mung bean sprout is softer than its soybean counterpart, so it's delicious both raw or after a short blanch.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Conversely, soybean sprouts are comparatively larger and quite hearty.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Unless they're cooked somewhat, they have an undesirable texture and aroma.
- Takane (Publishing Department): We can use this to our advantage when adding flavor to a broth or a sauce.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Soups made by boiling soybean sprouts are often considered to be some of the most delectable options for breakfast. They retain a striking and enjoyable flavor profile.
- "You really do critique everything..."
- Takane (Publishing Department): This stays between us, Sensei, so listen well.
- Takane (Publishing Department): To survive in this modern era, a critic must constantly be ready to articulate her educated opinion!
- Takane (Publishing Department): A professional critic must always be prepared to rouse public discourse! This is to be expected!
- Takane (Publishing Department): Ahem. I suppose I got a little carried away. I'm sorry about that.
- Takane (Publishing Department): My apologies, Sensei.
- "It's fine."
- Takane (Publishing Department): Shall we move on to today's topic, the Bean Sprout Festival?
- Takane (Publishing Department): For the festival, I usually stir-fry spicy soybean sprouts and pork!
- Takane (Publishing Department): I start with slightly-cooked soybean sprouts, then I add accentuating spices and pork before stir-frying the whole thing.
- Takane (Publishing Department): The soybean sprouts' flavor adds depth to the pork while the sauce and the fat rendered from the pork coat the bean sprouts...
- Takane (Publishing Department): That harmony makes this a perfect dish for cold winter days like this one. It's a symphony of calories, vitamins, and fiber!
- "Oh, that's exciting."
- Takane (Publishing Department): Indeed. Please give me a moment!
- Takane (Publishing Department): The dish is ready, Sensei!
- Takane (Publishing Department): You can have this over rice, or as a side dish!
- Takane (Publishing Department): Or would you prefer bread? If you prefer a more "hands-on experience," we can treat it as a sandwich filling. I recommend black bread!
- Takane (Publishing Department): What? Well... I am a mere student of Red Winter after all...far from professional.
- Takane (Publishing Department): But if you're enjoying it, know that there's plenty left. Please help yourself!
