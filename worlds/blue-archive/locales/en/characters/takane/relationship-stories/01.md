# Takane — Relationship Story 1

Original English wiki story transcript. No Korean dialogue is translated.

Source: https://bluearchive.wiki/wiki/Takane/Relationship_Story/1
Status: source-extracted-unreviewed


- Takane (Publishing Department): How do you do, Sensei? I am Miyoshi Takane, and I'd like to offer my greetings.
- "Welcome, Takane. This is your first time visiting Schale?"
- Takane (Publishing Department): Fufu. Indeed it is. Thank you for the warm welcome. Schale is as wonderful as they say.
- Takane (Publishing Department): And, speaking of "warm"... This weather is quite balmy, isn't it? I'm not sure I've ever experienced such a climate.
- Takane (Publishing Department): I apologize if this is too sudden, but...from the moment I started my journey to Schale, I was assailed by a certain curiosity.
- "About what?"
- Takane (Publishing Department): Well I...
- Takane (Publishing Department): I wish to learn more about you.
- "Wh-Where's this suddenly coming from?"
- Takane (Publishing Department): Well... Do you also not wish to learn more about ME, Sensei?
- Takane (Publishing Department): I'm certain that you do. Every teacher must take care to understand their students, do they not?
- Takane (Publishing Department): Very well. We are in accordance. From now on, we shall dedicate this time to learn more about one another.
- Takane (Publishing Department): After all, a critic must take care to intimately know whatever she's critiquing.
- "You critique people too?!"
- Takane (Publishing Department): Hmm... Technically, that's not something I would formally write and publish.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Regardless, the act of learning more about a subject, analyzing it, and discovering its hidden...value...is quite paramount.
- Takane (Publishing Department): I think both critiques and human relationships have that in common.
- Takane (Publishing Department): In that sense, if I take care with my words, I imagine that I can critique people one day.
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...With positive and productive intentions, of course.
- "Eloquent as always..."
- Takane (Publishing Department): Of course I am.
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...If I'm not, in this "digital age"... I'd never be able to make a living as a writer.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Now then, Sensei. Shall we discuss this book? I found it under your desk.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Perhaps I should read it with you and we can critique its merits and themes together?
- "Not that one, you won't!"
- Takane (Publishing Department): Wh-Why not...?
- Takane (Publishing Department): No matter what, I am "explicitly" not to read or critique this book...?
- Takane (Publishing Department): Hm...
- Takane (Publishing Department): All right, then. Keep your secrets.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Still, I confess a spot of disappointment... You know, Sensei, I can be quite open-minded.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Are you so certain I'm not allowed?
- "...You can read it over my dead body."
- Takane (Publishing Department): Ah, could it truly be so dire?
- Takane (Publishing Department): Nevertheless, I have noticed an...eclectic collection of items arranged around your office.
- Takane (Publishing Department): It's such a varied motley that I can't uncover a cohesive theme or thesis.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Oh? Is that so? These are items your dear students gifted you? What a quaint array of souvenirs, so haphazardly stacked about.
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...
- Takane (Publishing Department): And while that's quite "sweet," I must insist that you organize them with SOME semblance of order.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Classification and arrangement should demonstrate thoughtfulness. The way you arrange things demonstrates value in the act itself.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Further, by doing so, you would assist me in my attempts to understand what sort of person you are.
- Takane (Publishing Department): So, in the spirit of that, I present a gift.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Here you will find this month's Kivotos cultural critiques published by our Publishing Department.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Oh, do feel free to skip pages 22 to 24.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Those pages contain Meruri's..."creative" corner.
- "Do you publish this every month, Takane?"
- Takane (Publishing Department): I'm not the sole writer, but I do my best to fill the pages allotted to me.
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...Although, in times when there isn't anything worth critiquing, I sometimes am forced to acquiesce with a heavy heart...
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...Indeed. If we solely pursued analog publication, we would have never survived.
- Takane (Publishing Department): As we continue in the publishing and cultural enrichment industry, we try to expand into other areas, anything that will help make ends meet.
- Takane (Publishing Department): I feel like the tail is wagging the dog now, so I would personally prefer things to go back to the days when we were solely handling published works, but...
- Takane (Publishing Department): *sigh* I understand that I'm coming across as ungrateful. Even I must concede that the president's business strategy was a success.
- Takane (Publishing Department): And so, I keep coming to a similar conclusion.
- Takane (Publishing Department): If I have time to complain about how I must compromise in order to keep my job, then it is also my duty to make good critiques for the greater public...
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...so that I can unearth hidden gems of value and present them to as many people as possible.
- Takane (Publishing Department): So, Sensei! Now that we're on the topic...
- Takane (Publishing Department): Could you reveal to me the sides of you that you normally keep hidden?
- "Teachers don't keep secrets from their students..."
- Takane (Publishing Department): Wow, what a textbook response. It sounds almost coached.
- Takane (Publishing Department): But I've already uncovered two things, you realize?
- Takane (Publishing Department): I know that the gifts students give you carry a great weight of sentimentality.
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...And that even YOU have something you want to hide from your students. I have an inkling of what it is.
- "Th-Then I think it's your turn to show me your hidden side!"
- Takane (Publishing Department): Of course. Feel free to ask any and all questions.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Oh, but please refrain from prying into sensitive topics...
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...
- Takane (Publishing Department): Depending on the question, I may need to censor myself.
- "I-I'm not going to ask anything weird like that!"
- Takane (Publishing Department): R-Really...?
- Takane (Publishing Department): What would you like to learn about, then?
- Takane (Publishing Department): What? My hobbies? You mean, other than the obvious answer of "reading"?
- Takane (Publishing Department): Hm... I don't enjoy the thought of you finding me so trite and predictable.
- Takane (Publishing Department): It's even worse because your guess was absolutely correct.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Fufu... But even I have a hobby that will no doubt raise your eyebrows.
- Takane (Publishing Department): It's ice hockey!
- "Ice hockey?!"
- Takane (Publishing Department): Yes! Ice hockey is a grand sport! How much do you know of ice hockey, Sensei?
- Takane (Publishing Department): R-Really? Then, I'm curious...
- Takane (Publishing Department): Do you also subscribe to online channels, have a favored team you earnestly cheer for, and meticulously analyze the performance of rival teams?!
- Takane (Publishing Department): Oh, not that much then... In that case, Sensei...
- Takane (Publishing Department): Oh yes. That makes a lot of sense.
- Takane (Publishing Department): It's always so cold in Red Winter, so we are always playing the traditional "winter sports." I understand that is not the truth elsewhere.
- Takane (Publishing Department): Then perhaps, Sensei...
- Takane (Publishing Department): Would you be interested in watching an ice hockey game with me the next time you visit Red Winter?
- Takane (Publishing Department): I will be happy to commentate...
- Takane (Publishing Department): ...No, I'll even offer critiques!
