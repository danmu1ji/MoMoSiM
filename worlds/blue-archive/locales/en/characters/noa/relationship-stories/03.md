> Cafe Near Millennium School
> I stopped at a nearby cafe on my way back from work.
> ...And spotted Noa contemplating deeply over a pile of paperwork.
- "...Noa?"
- Noa (Seminar): Oh, Sensei.
- Noa (Seminar): Sorry I didn't notice you, I was absorbed in my work.
- "So, what's the work that requires so much of your attention?"
- Noa (Seminar): One of my duties as Seminar's secretary is to review patents.
- Noa (Seminar): The Millennium Prize is coming up, so everyone at Millennium is kicking it into high gear, which includes submitting plenty of patent applications.
- Noa (Seminar): Evaluating and arranging these patents, both during and after application, are important parts of my job.
- Noa (Seminar): I usually work in the club room, but the weather is so nice today, and I wanted to go somewhere I could enjoy it.
- Noa (Seminar): Catching up on work under a warm sun is its own sort of cozy.
- "Is there anything I can help you with?"
- Noa (Seminar): No need.
- Noa (Seminar): This is my job, but it's also something I genuinely enjoy.
- Noa (Seminar): Unless... Sensei, you're not still trying to break my composure under the pretext of helping me, are you? Heehee.
- Noa (Seminar): Feel free to sit. Don't worry about me and enjoy your time.
- Noa (Seminar): Feel free to. Don't worry about me and enjoy your time.
- Noa (Seminar): Or...perhaps you could try observing my every move again?
- "I'm good on record-keeping for now..."
- Noa (Seminar): Heehee, just kidding. Unlike me, you don't seem to be on the clock anyway.
- Noa (Seminar): Speaking of which... I need to get back to this paperwork.
> As time went on, the neat stacks of reviewed papers climbed ever higher.
- Noa (Seminar): This one...to the left... This one...also left...
- "How are you categorizing the documents?"
- Noa (Seminar): They are sorted according to whether or not the potential patents meet the basic requirements for consideration.
- Noa (Seminar): Proposals that meet the requirements are placed on the right. Those that don't are placed on the left.
- "Then all these documents on the left are..."
- Noa (Seminar): Yes. These are proposals that don't meet the basic requirements and will be discarded.
- Noa (Seminar): Unfortunately, there are various reasons that not all ideas can be registered as patents.
- Noa (Seminar): But success is born from failure. If these failures keep piling up, it might result in a game-changing invention, or maybe even a paradigm-shifting one.
- Look through the rejected documents.
- Noa (Seminar): You're interested in the Unlimited Boiler Works Engine that has 99.99% heat transfer efficiency?
- Noa (Seminar): An interesting proposal, to be sure, but it can't be patented because the invention isn't actually complete.
- Noa (Seminar): The idea of coating the inside with highly insulating material is sound, but the actual materials and method of such insulation were not presented.
- "And this is...?"
- Noa (Seminar): A research report from the New Material Development Club. While researching improved adhesives, they somehow synthesized a new explosive substance.
- Noa (Seminar): The discovery is fascinating, but discovery alone doesn't guarantee a patent.
- Noa (Seminar): A patent isn't just new knowledge, it is the use and specific application of that knowledge.
- "What about this one?"
- Noa (Seminar): In the case of the mask that can whiten skin and teeth at the same time...
- Noa (Seminar): The patent is perfectly fine...except for the fact that the same thing was already patented by the Engineering Department last month.
- Noa (Seminar): At the end of the day, patents are about ownership, and in the world of patents, two owners cannot exist.
- "The world of patents is profound..."
- Noa (Seminar): It can seem cutthroat, but patents are vital in ensuring that the actual creators get due credit.
- Noa (Seminar): If patents aren't processed fairly, people can abuse the system, which discourages creators from pursuing new ideas.
- Noa (Seminar): Hmm... This...
- Noa (Seminar): Well, this patent application could still make it through.
- "Why the initial uncertainty?"
- Noa (Seminar): It's an unusual invention, but it satisfies the basic requirements.
- Noa (Seminar): In fact, I think it would be very useful to Millennium.
- Noa (Seminar): Would you like to take a look, Sensei?
- Noa (Seminar): Heehee. Interesting, isn't it?
- Noa (Seminar): Heehee. Interesting, isn't it?
- Noa (Seminar): Predictive analysis based on behavioral patterns and tendencies isn't a novel idea in and of itself...
- Noa (Seminar): But this is the first time that it's been applied to a single person, and a teacher no less. It doesn't violate any existing patents, and the proposal is very...comprehensive.
- Noa (Seminar): And while it's not my job to judge the commercial viability of a patent...
- Noa (Seminar): I believe there is plenty of demand in Kivotos for a device that could track you, right?
- Noa (Seminar): Oh my, is that an objection?
- Noa (Seminar): Oh my, is that an objection?
- Noa (Seminar): You can appeal for the invalidation of a patent, of course, but...
- Noa (Seminar): Verbal requests are invalid. Please apply using the proper channels and documentation.
> After that, we had a heated debate over whether or not the patent was valid. Somehow, I feel like I lost.
