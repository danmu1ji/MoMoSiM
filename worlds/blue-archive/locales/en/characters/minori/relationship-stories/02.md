# Minori — Relationship Story 2

> Red Winter Academy Library
> I stopped by Red Winter Academy's library on Schale business, hoping to pull some administrative records.
> I bumped into Minori there. She was lost in the stacks, engrossed in a book.
- Minori (Labor Party): Hmm...
- "Minori?"
- Minori (Labor Party): Oh, Sensei? What are you doing at the academy library?
- Minori (Labor Party): I hope you're not looking for the academy's administrative records...
- Minori (Labor Party): I wouldn't bother. Most of Red Winter Academy's records are redacted and changed whenever we have a coup, so there aren't many originals left.
- Minori (Labor Party): You might have better luck checking the Knowledge Liberation Front's selection. Those are mostly intact.
- Minori (Labor Party): They even have old, out-of-print novels. So if you have a book in mind, you should stop by.
- "Thanks for that. But why are you here, Minori?"
- Minori (Labor Party): What am I doing. Here. In the library.
- Minori (Labor Party): I'm here to read books. I thought it'd be obvious...
- Minori (Labor Party): You don't think I'm like that child from the Secretariat, who hides out in the library because it's a good place for a nap?
- Minori (Labor Party): I guess the Labor Party has kind of a rough image. But to do good work, you need good manners and learning.
- Minori (Labor Party): And how are we supposed to understand our work contracts if we aren't decent readers?
- Minori (Labor Party): That's why it's important for me to keep up my reading. As president of the Labor Party.
- Minori (Labor Party): When I'm not working or striking, I'm often here in the library, reading. It helps that it's warmer than most of the other buildings on campus.
- "What kind of books do you like to read?"
- Minori (Labor Party): Well, sci-fi and essays always get me thinking. But my favorite would have to be class literature.
- "Class literature."
- Minori (Labor Party): You know. Literature written for the enlightenment and emancipation of the working class! Literature that agitates against the capital elite!
- Minori (Labor Party): As a worker, I find it very relatable. And it teaches me how to deal with capitalists!
- "I-I see..."
- Minori (Labor Party): There are so many books to choose from, but let's see... My favorites...
- Minori (Labor Party): The Fox and the Crane.
- "Um... That's Aesop, right?"
- Minori (Labor Party): Ah, you've heard of it! Well, it is in the library of most schools, so that makes sense.
- Minori (Labor Party): It's a classic of guerrilla literature, in which the working-class crane uses its wits to wage a revolution against the sly capitalist fox.
- Minori (Labor Party): The fox promises to share its food equally, but it serves the food on a flat plate that the crane can't eat out of, thereby hoarding resources for itself...
- "Is that how it goes...?"
- Minori (Labor Party): Yes, it is.
- Minori (Labor Party): But the crane learns from this, see? So when the fox comes to the crane's house, it serves dinner in a narrow jug. The fox can't get at the food, thus ensuring victory for the crane!
- Minori (Labor Party): It's a fable, see? The moral of the story is that you must lure your enemy into an environment where you hold the advantage!
- "(I don't think that's what Aesop meant...)"
- Minori (Labor Party): What else... Oh, right, The Three Little Pigs!
- Minori (Labor Party): This book is essential reading for any worker in construction. It deals with lessons about on-site safety, recording two cases of failure and one case of success.
- Minori (Labor Party): The first and second pigs ignored civic codes and used cheap materials, and their houses collapsed just from a strong wind! The third pig, however, used approved materials, and his house was safe.
- Minori (Labor Party): Of course, it would have been better if he framed out the house in rebar and concrete first...
- Minori (Labor Party): But it's a children's story, so it can be forgiven for going light on the details.
- "That's, uh, very generous of you..."
- Minori (Labor Party): Ah, and I can't forget about The North Wind and the Sun!
- Minori (Labor Party): The winds and the sun conspire to take a poor traveler's cloak through a combination of hard and soft tactics, just like how the capitalist class divides workers and tricks them into fighting each other!
- Minori (Labor Party): The moral is that the worker must never, ever let her guard down, and always keep the fighting spirit of—
> I listened to Minori's unique takes on old fables until the sun was low in the sky...
