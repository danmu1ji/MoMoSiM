> Gehenna Outskirts Teahouse
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): I've been waiting for you, Sensei.
- "This place is incredible..."
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Heh. Research time is important, so I wanted the best. I spare no expense when it comes to research.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): We can research in peace—the entire place is ours until midnight.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): What better place than a teahouse late at night?
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Let's get started, then.
> *thump*
> (Haruna put a cup of instant ramen on the table.)
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Today's epicurean research subject is instant cup ramen.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Today's epicurean research subject is instant cup ramen.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Huh? Why not have a tea ceremony?
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): There's been a misunderstanding. I never said we'd be researching tea ceremonies today.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): All I said was that tea ceremonies teach the ultimate form of etiquette.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Consider the following. If posture, atmosphere, demeanor, and mindset determine how we perceive what we taste...
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): ...what would happen if we apply the same philosophy when eating cup ramen?
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): In other words, instant cup ramen...
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): ...is worthy of being researched!
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Heh heh. Very good! I knew you'd understand its profoundness!
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Heh heh. Very good! I knew you'd understand its profoundness!
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Now, let me explain what we'll be using for this experiment.
> *thump*
> (Haruna takes out an expensive-looking pot.)
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): This pottery was made by an artisan. I'll be using it to boil our water.
> (Haruna takes out a small water bottle.)
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): This is artesian water from over 500 meters underground in Gehenna's volcanic region.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): It's very expensive mineral water. And I mean very.
> (Haruna takes out a large basket.)
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): This is charcoal made from oak—yet another expensive item. It will help the water boil to an even temperature.
> *tick* *tick* *tick*
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): I also have a stopwatch to measure the time, a thermometer to measure the temperature of the water, a pair of the finest bamboo chopsticks, and, finally, a drip pot to pour the water...
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Heh heh. Everything looks good. Shall we start?
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Allow me to boil the water.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Let's see...how do I light the charcoal?
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): *cough* *cough* Huh? There's no fire—just smoke...!
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Is this how a furnace is supposed to work? How am I supposed to use the pottery...?
> (It took us two hours to light the charcoal. Finally, she put water in the pot, boiled it, and brought the boiled water in the drip pot.)
> (That was when I saw the electric kettle in the corner of the teahouse, but decided not to say anything.)
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Heh. Thanks for waiting. It's ready to go.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Slow down. We're only getting started!
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Slow down. We're only getting started!
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): We still have to decide how to pour the water and if the hot water should touch the noodles or the powdered seasoning first!
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): I was thinking of going with the hand-drip method. There are different patterns for pouring water, such as the spiral, circle, and dots, but I'll go with the spiral pour...!
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): This is how I first learned to make hand-drip coffee, so it should work.
> *trickle*
> (I have no choice but to silently watch Haruna pour the water with great concentration.)
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): *pant* *pant*
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Pouring water requires so much concentration.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): That should do. I'll set three minutes on the stopwatch...
> (Three minutes later.)
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Here you go, Sensei!
> (Haruna and I ate our 100-yen instant cup ramen in the teahouse late at night.)
> (...It tasted good, just like ordinary ramen should.)
> On the Way Home
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Heh heh... What did you think of today's research?
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): I thought it was rather tasty—different from ordinary ramen.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Eating ramen in an elegant space like a quiet and peaceful teahouse...
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): I wondered if what I felt in that moment was enlightenment.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): I think we can perfect the Tao of cup ramen with a little more experimentation.
- Haruna (Gourmet Research Society): Heh... But that's enough for today. See you later!
> (I saw Haruna off. She seemed very satisfied as we parted ways.)
