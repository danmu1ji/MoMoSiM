> Gehenna Library 17
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): What do you think is the most important skill an executive can have, Sensei?
- "Charisma to keep employees motivated?"
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Covering the basics is important. That's true.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): But there's something even more important.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): No, of course not! It doesn't need that!
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): No, of course not! It doesn't need that!
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Listen up, Sensei. The skill a leader needs the most is...
- "...Is?"
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): ...the power of persuasion—the ability to turn any opponent into an ally!
- "Isn't that also one of the basics?"
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Simple is best! You can't do anything without mastering the fundamentals.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Anyway, you won't get anywhere in business if you can't persuade others.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): The one who strikes first gains the advantage, no matter what realm of business it is.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): And since I'm the boss around here, that means I already have a knack for persuading others.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): You're the one here who needs to learn the power of persuasion, Sensei.
- "Me?"
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): We're business partners, remember? You need to expand your capabilities as a co-owner.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Let's try a hypothetical.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Say you were a candidate applying to join Problem Solver...
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): ...and I'll be the one conducting your interview.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): This book covers all kinds of methods of persuasion.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Glean everything you can from it and use what you learn on me.
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): I want you to convince me to hire you at our agency.
> Like Aru said, the book detailed multiple persuasion techniques.
> The first method was the art of give and take.
> "It takes two to tango. If you want something from your opponent, give them something first so that they feel like they're in your debt."
- "How about I take you out for ramen, Aru? We can discuss my contract then."
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): D-Dinner with Sensei?!
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Yup! That'll do! You're hired!
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): ...Wait, that's not right! You can't just bribe your way into a job with ramen!
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): That's not going to work. Disqualified!
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Fool me once, shame on you. Let's see you try again, Sensei.
> The second method of persuasion was the art of listening.
> "To earn someone's favor, first listen to what they have to say and then give your opinion."
- "You're always busy, Aru. Bosses must have a lot of responsibilities."
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): I guess that's true. I resolve our clients' claims every week all by myself.
- "Do you need someone to talk to?"
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): It wouldn't hurt.
- "Then, you should hire me."
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Oh, really?
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): ...Wait, did you think I would hire you so easily?!
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Don't patronize me! It takes a lot more than that to keep me happy!
- "I think I could be very helpful to you, Aru."
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): D-Don't you think you're coming on a little strong?
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Ugh. Where is this convincing aura coming from? It's so strong!
- Aru (Problem Solver 68): Sensei... You've mastered the art of persuasion!
> After that, Aru kept giving me mock interviews and tested me on the other methods in the book.
> I was "hired" twenty four times in twelve hours.
