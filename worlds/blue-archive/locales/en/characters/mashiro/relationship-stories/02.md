> Trinity Classroom
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): Justice... It rolls off the tongue so easily. Is there any better word in existence?
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): The Justice Task Force has always striven to uphold justice in Kivotos, and those who commit injustice are thoroughly punished.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): I once fought believing everything my upperclassmen did was just and that the opposite was true for our enemies from Gehenna...but lately, I've had doubts.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): Th-That isn't to say I've lost faith in my upperclassmen!
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): However, once they graduate, the Justice Task Force will be left to me, and I'll have to use my own judgment to determine what is just.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): To do so, I need to have a clear understanding of what justice is.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): So I went out and picked up a copy of this best selling classic: "Justice: It's the Right Thing to Do".
- "I've never heard of that one..."
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): It was everything I hoped it would be. It covered several examples of justice and injustice and how they applied to several situations.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): For example, bullies who torment others are unjust, whereas doctors who save others from painful disease are just. Delicious food that brings joy to people is just, but bizarre food that causes stomach pain is unjust.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): Everything felt so clear and straightforward. It spoke to my personal sense of justice.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): But a few of the examples I still struggle to understand due to my inexperience.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): Would you mind listening to them and telling me what the right thing to do is?
- "Sure, lay it on me."
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): Okay. Here's the first example.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): "You are a train engineer. One day, you see five people tied to the track you're about to enter."
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): "If you switch to another track, you can save the five people, but as a result, you will hit one other person. What, in this case, would be the right thing to do?"
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): It's difficult to answer. Switching to another track would be the right thing if I'm trying to save more people, but doing so requires sacrificing someone else who was otherwise not in harm's way.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): What's more important? Saving lives or not willfully harming another?
- "What does the book have to say?"
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): It says...that this is a thought experiment, so there is no answer.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): Justice without an answer? What nonsense.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): Justice should always have a clear, singular answer...
- "What would you choose to do?"
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): Hmm. Is there no way to save them all?
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): I can't accept that answer, even if it goes against the spirit of the scenario.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): I would do my best to save all of them, no matter what it took.
- Mashiro (Justice Task Force): That is the way of justice!
> Mashiro and I had a good time reading over the book together.
